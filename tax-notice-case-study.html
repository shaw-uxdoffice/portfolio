01 — Context & Background

High-stakes calls, fragmented tools

ADP associates support hundreds of thousands of business clients on payroll tax. When those clients receive tax agency notices — from the IRS, state agencies, or unemployment bureaus — they call ADP expecting clarity, not more confusion.


Zone is ADP's enterprise support platform, built on Salesforce CRM. It's where associates live during every client interaction. The guided flow needed to embed within Zone. Associates are on live calls under time pressure. Every extra click, every system switch, every ambiguous decision costs the client.


The scale is significant. ADP manages payroll tax for over 300,000 small business clients with state and local jurisdictions. A tax notice can mean anything from a missed deposit to a rate change to a legal name mismatch — and the associate on the call often has seconds, not minutes, to classify which one.

02 — The Problem to Solve

Two associates, same notice, different decisions

The core failure was not a technology gap — it was a decision-support gap. Associates determined ownership, validated details, and choose to escalate or resolve based on training memory and personal judgment. The result was inconsistency at scale.


Research teams were the visible victims: they received cases that were incomplete, invalid, or could have been resolved on the initial call. Each avoidable escalation added 10 to 20 days to a client's wait. For a small business staring at a penalty notice, that delay had real financial consequences.

Problem Statement
"During live, high-stress client calls, service associates lacked a standardized workflow to validate payroll tax notices. This resulted in inconsistent case intake, increased cognitive load that impacted handling times, and avoidable escalations that delayed case resolution to 10–20 days, compounding client anxiety and exposing businesses to costly regulatory penalties."

04 — Discovery & Research

Understanding the call in real time

Discovery focused on two things: mapping what associates actually did during tax notice calls, and identifying where the process broke down — not in theory, but in the specific moment of a live client interaction.


Research inputs included associate interview, stakeholder conversations, process documentation, existing workflow diagrams, Salesforce/Zone case handling patterns, SME walkthroughs, and research team intake requirements. The goal wasn't to document the full policy — it was to find the decision points that caused inconsistency.


The critical insight: most associate uncertainty clustered around the same questions. Is this ADP's period? Is the jurisdiction set up? Does the Tax ID match? Does ADP have authorization? These weren't rare edge cases — they were the standard path, and they had no in-platform support.

Key Questions That Shaped the Design
Q Which notice types do associates handle most — and where does each one go wrong?
Q What must be validated before a case is valid to escalate?
Q Where do associates currently get stuck, and what do they reach for when they do?
Q What does a "valid escalation" mean for each notice type, from the research team's perspective?
Q Which cases could have been resolved on the first call — and weren't?

05 — Hypothesis & Design Principles

Validation first, escalation second

The central design hypothesis was about sequencing, not simplification. The tax rules themselves didn't need to be hidden — they needed to be revealed in the right order, at the right moment, through the right question.

The guided flow's core value was not speed. It was consistency. An associate who is confident in their decision — because the platform walked them through validation — serves the client better than an associate who is fast but uncertain. That insight shaped every design decision that followed.

Core Hypothesis
"If associates are guided through a structured tax notice validation flow inside Zone, then they will determine responsibility more consistently, resolve eligible cases during the call, reduce unnecessary escalations, and give clients clearer expectations — without requiring associates to memorize every exception or rule."
Associates wanted guidance, not hand-holding
Experienced users needed the flexibility to skip steps or take shortcuts, while new users needed more support.
Context switching killed productivity
Every time users had to leave the flow to check documentation, they lost momentum and increased error rates.
Visual cues beat text explanations
Users responded better to visual indicators and progressive disclosure than lengthy instructions.
Design principles
01
Structured Guidence
Step-by-step flows that reduce cognitive load while maintaining transparency
02
Determine ADP responsibility early
Guide associates through the steps to determine issue responsibility — whether it is ADP-owned, client-owned or agency-owned.
03
Plain Language Over Jargon
Internal terms are contextualised, not exposed raw. Clear instructions, helper text, and "Knowledge bites" replace acronyms with meaning — without removing the underlying logic.
04
Designed for Live Call Pressure
Short prompts. Clear next steps. Minimal backtracking. No question asks the associate to leave Zone to find the answer. Every gate is resolvable within the context of the current screen.
05
Capture Once, Reuse Downstream
Information entered during the guided flow populates the Salesforce case object. The associate does not re-enter data. The research team receives a complete record, not a blank intake form.
06
Sequence Complexity, Don't Hide It
Tax notice handling is genuinely complex. The design doesn't oversimplify — it reveals the right complexity at the right time. Associates encounter difficulty when they're ready for it, with context.

06 — The Proposed Solution

Eight gates that changed the routing logic

The Tax Notice Guided Flow embedded directly within Zone — no context switching, no separate tool. It guides associates through ownership determination, validation gates, structured notice classification, and either in-call resolution or qualified escalation.

The validation gate framework is the intellectual core of the solution. Each gate asks a specific question that determines whether the case can continue, needs correction, or should stop. The gates are not bureaucratic checkboxes — they are the exact questions that tax SMEs said must be answered before a case is valid to escalate. The design made those questions unavoidable.

07 — Design Evolution & Concepts Not Pursued

Ideas tested, and good reasons to stop

Each discarded concept revealed something important about the real problem — and pushed the solution toward the validation-gate framework.

Enterprise design constraints are real. The solution had to live inside Zone, work with Salesforce case objects, support associates with varying experience levels, and handle compliance-critical decisions without automation risk. That combination ruled out several initially appealing directions.

01
Concept Discarded

Tree structure Jusrisdiction Selection

Jurisdiction data from state and local agencies is highly unstructured and inconsistently formatted across federal, state, and local levels, making it unreliable.

02
Concept Discarded

Automating the validation of associate input against system records

Business rejected fully automated validation due to high agency-data variability, potential system latency, and the critical need for human-in-the-loop compliance. Incorrect automation poses significant regulatory risks and undermines the transparency required during high-stakes client calls. Instead, we adopted Guided Manual Validation, where the system surfaces contextual data while the associate confirms findings, ensuring each decision is accurate and explainable.

03
Concept Discarded

Modern conversational experience

Prioritized a structured interface to maximize technical feasibility and reduce development costs, as internal tools derive more value from streamlined task completion than stunning interface. Current task-centric design leverages existing associate workflows to ensure familiarity and minimize training time.

08 — Impact & Outcomes

09 — Reflections & Learnings

What this project changed about how I design

01

Guided Workflows Are Most Valuable When the Stakes Are Highest

This project refined a core belief: the time to structure a decision process is before the situation becomes stressful, not after. On a live client call about a penalty notice, an associate's working memory is occupied. The design's job is to hold the process so the associate can hold the conversation. Any interface that requires the user to remember the next step has already failed under pressure.

02

Validation Logic Is a UX Artifact — Not Just an Engineering Concern

In enterprise compliance, I learned that information architecture and decision logic—not screen design—are the true frontiers of critical design thinking, where the sequence of validation gates directly dictates operational accuracy and business outcomes.

03

Internal Terminology Is a Workflow Problem, Not a Content Problem

Reframing complex terminology as a structural design challenge—rather than a cosmetic one—enabled me to eliminate decision gaps and standardize case outcomes through contextual guidance.

04

Not Every Decision Should Be Automated — Especially Not Compliance Decisions

The temptation to automate ownership determination was real. It would have felt like a more sophisticated solution. But in a compliance workflow, incorrect automation creates risk that no UX polish can recover from. Decision support — structured guidance that combines system data with human judgment — was the right model. The associate remains accountable, but the platform removes the inconsistency. That distinction matters: it protects the client, the associate, and the firm.

05

The Best Enterprise Solutions Reduce Work for Multiple Groups Simultaneously

A design that helps one group and burdens another is not a solution — it is a redistribution of friction. The validation gate framework served associates, clients, and research teams at the same time. Associates had structure. Clients had faster clarity. Research teams had complete cases. That alignment happened because the design started from a shared definition of what a "valid escalation" meant — not from a single user's perspective, but from the full workflow. That systems thinking is what I carry forward into every enterprise project.

"The UI must do the thinking for them. Our associates shouldn't need to remember steps—the system should guide them through the right sequence every time."
